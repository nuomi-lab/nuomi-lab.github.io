# **Resources**

Resources for prospective and current students.

## For Prospective Students

- **[How to join NUOMI Lab](/#joining)**: current openings, what we look for in applicants, and how to get in touch.
- **[What research is (and isn't)](/resources/research-skills#what-research-is)**: common misconceptions about research, and reasons you might want to do it. A good place to start if you are considering a PhD.

## For Current Students

We have put together a set of materials to guide you in developing concrete research skills, collected on our **[Research Skills](/resources/research-skills)** page. Good places to start:

- **[Four Fundamental Skills of Research](/resources/research-skills#fundamental-skills)**: defining and scoping a research problem, surveying related work, sustaining progress, and communicating results, with what we expect for each.
- **[Best Research Practices](/resources/research-skills#best-practices)**: how to make the most of your research meetings, your research week, and your code.
- **[How to Write a Machine Learning Research Paper](/resources/research-skills#technical-ideas)**: how to go from a one-sentence-per-point introduction to a polished paper.
- **[Skills for a Research Career](/resources/research-skills#career-skills)**: a map of the skills researchers keep developing, from managing research to managing yourself.

## Further Reading

- **[How to communicate about research progress](https://mogu-lab.github.io/tutorials/communication-in-collaborative-environments)** (from CS290): how good communication with collaborators gets you unstuck and leads to clearer, more structured feedback on your work.
- **[How to read a research paper](https://mogu-lab.github.io/tutorials/how-to-read-a-research-paper)** (from CS290 and AM207): strategies for reading dense papers that assume a lot of background knowledge.
- **[How to make the most of your PhD](https://yanivyacoby.github.io/a-guide-to-your-phd/)**: a guide from current to incoming PhD students on the "hidden curriculum" of doctoral programs, useful for graduate students in general.
