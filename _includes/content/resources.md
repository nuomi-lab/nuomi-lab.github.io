# **Resources**

Resources for prospective and current students.

## For Prospective Students

- **[How to join NUOMI Lab](/#joining)**: current openings, what we look for in applicants, and how to get in touch.
- **[What research is (and isn't)](/resources/research-skills#what-research-is)**: common misconceptions about research, and reasons you might want to do it. A good place to start if you are considering a PhD.

## For Current Students

We have put together a set of materials to guide you in developing concrete research skills, collected on our **[Research Skills](/resources/research-skills)** page. Good places to start:

- **[Four Fundamental Skills of Research](/resources/research-skills#fundamental-skills)**: defining and scoping a research problem, surveying related work, sustaining progress, and communicating results, with what we expect for each.
- **[Research Best Practices](/resources/research-skills#best-practices)**: how to make the most of your research meetings, your research week, and your code.
- **[How to Write a Machine Learning Research Paper](/resources/research-skills#technical-ideas)**: how to go from a one-sentence-per-point introduction to a polished paper.
- **[Skills for a Research Career](/resources/research-skills#career-skills)**: a map of the skills researchers keep developing, from managing research to managing yourself.

For the technical foundations of our work:

- **[Notes for Introduction to Machine Learning](https://onefishy.github.io/ML_notes/)**: notes by Weiwei Pan for the introduction to machine learning course that they taught at Harvard. These notes cover regression and classification, neural networks, Bayesian modelling and inference, and latent-variable models, and treat uncertainty, interpretation and responsible use as part of machine learning, not only prediction.
- **[Probabilistic Inference & Optimization](https://onefishy.github.io/probabilistic-inference-and-optimization/)**: a textbook by Weiwei Pan based on the course on foundations of probabilistic machine learning that they taught at Harvard for five years. It covers Bayesian modelling, Markov chain Monte Carlo, variational inference and stochastic optimization, with an emphasis on implementation.

## Further Reading
The following are materials for research student that we co-developed with [MOGU Lab](https://mogu-lab.github.io/): 
- **[How to communicate about research progress](https://mogu-lab.github.io/tutorials/communication-in-collaborative-environments)**: how good communication with collaborators gets you unstuck and leads to clearer, more structured feedback on your work.
- **[How to read a research paper](https://mogu-lab.github.io/tutorials/how-to-read-a-research-paper)**: strategies for reading dense papers that assume a lot of background knowledge.
- **[How to make the most of your PhD](https://yanivyacoby.github.io/a-guide-to-your-phd/)**: a guide from current to incoming PhD students on the "hidden curriculum" of doctoral programs, useful for graduate students in general.
