# **What Research Is (and Isn't)**

Most of us are exposed to research through depictions in popular culture and media, which often default to tropes (e.g. the mad scientist, the wunderkind, the lone tortured genius) that encode misconceptions about the real practices of scientific research. In reality, research is much more mundane, and much more accessible and rewarding, than common misconceptions suggest.

1. **"Research is done by a lone genius, usually by deriving lots of math."** In reality, research is almost always done in a team of collaborators who bring different skills, interests, and perspectives (and personalities). Most questions can be tackled fruitfully in many different ways, not just by doing lots of fancy math.

2. **"Research is done by making monumental breakthroughs from epiphanies."** In reality, research is done by iterating: asking very concrete, narrowly scoped questions each week, designing experiments to answer these questions, then generating new questions.

3. **"The value of research is in the eyes of reviewers at prestigious conferences and journals."** In reality, the value of research is what it means to you. You need to find an independent reason for doing research.

4. **"Research skills are inborn; some people are just better at it."** In reality, no one is born with research skills, and everyone has to work hard to gain them.

5. **"Research is always fun, and driven by burning intellectual fire and moral passion."** In reality, research can sometimes be exciting and fun, but it can also be very frustrating. You need to find a sustainable reason for doing research.

## Why You Might Want to Do Research

You might want to do research for many different reasons; there is no single right reason to do research. For example:

- You are considering a PhD and want to know if you like research.
- You like working on open-ended problems.
- You like working with a team of friends on a challenge.
- You want to solve a real-world problem.
- You want to present your results at a machine learning workshop so you can meet other researchers in machine learning.
- You want to submit your results to a machine learning conference and connect with the wider machine learning community.
