# **Skills for a Research Career**

This is a superset of generally helpful skills for Machine Learning PhD students and researchers. No one is expected to have all of them, and researchers keep developing these skills throughout their careers. Use it as a map to reflect on where you are and what you'd like to work on next.

## Managing Research

- Time management: how to be efficient
- How to switch between different projects, or manage your time across different projects
- How to critically evaluate research proposals and questions
- How to read research papers (quickly)
- How to find relevant resources on Google (how to identify keywords, and know that certain keywords are related to your questions)
- How to design toy datasets to test hypotheses
- How to come up with hypotheses given results
- How to synthesize insight from a bunch of random results
- How to evaluate the novelty of your technical contribution to a body of literature
- How to pivot when a project seemingly reaches a dead end
- How to come up with small project ideas (extending existing work)
- How to come up with big project ideas (asking more big-picture or philosophical questions)
- How to formalize a vague question into a concrete, solvable problem statement
- How to break up a big research program into concrete questions for students you mentor

## Communicating Research

- How to communicate results during 10-, 20-, and 30-minute meetings
- How to ask for the feedback you need during research meetings
- How to communicate your research program as an elevator pitch
- How to listen for insight during technical talks
- How to phrase questions for Q&A during and after technical talks
- How to give talks to, and write for, different types of audiences
- How to write and speak in a mathematically and technically precise fashion
- How to review papers
- How to solicit feedback

## Managing Collaborations

- How to network
- How to set expectations for collaborations (authorship, workflow, roles, etc.)
- How to avoid or resolve potential conflicts during collaborations: e.g. making your collaborators feel appreciated and their contributions valued, and making sure that you feel your own role is well defined and your contribution significant and valued
- How to work effectively with people who are more senior, or more experienced or skilled in an area you're working in
- How to mentor younger researchers and students

## Being a Faculty Member

- How to identify what kind of institution you want to work at
- How to prepare a research statement
- How to prepare a teaching statement (if relevant)
- How to prepare a job talk
- What questions to ask during interviews to find out whether you want to work at that place
- How to answer interview questions
- Writing grants
- Balancing research and teaching
- Balancing research, teaching, and service

## Managing Yourself

- How to manage stress from lots of work
- How to manage work-life balance
- How to manage stress from not having a strong identity as a researcher, or from having that identity challenged
- How to manage stress from uncertainty about your progress
- How to manage stress from second-guessing feedback or your progress
- How to realistically evaluate your growth and progress
- How to motivate yourself to work (or to work effectively)
- How to set reasonable goals for yourself
